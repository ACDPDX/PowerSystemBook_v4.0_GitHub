Welcome to the simulation with my two toolboxes, the load flow calculation and transient simulation of high voltage networks. Today it's about the load flow calculation. Normally, this is not to be done with LT Spice because the complex alternating current analysis in LT spice is linear, but some elements in the load flow calculation are not linear.

This is especially true for loads that are modeled as constant power loads, and therefore do not depend linearly on the voltage To be able to calculate, nevertheless, the complex matrix system for load flow calculations is transferred in two real valued matrices. After that, the simulation can be carried out with the three nonlinear analyses in LTspice.

These are the op analysis operating point analysis. To determine the working point, the transient analysis for the simulation in timeline and the DC analysis thought for the direct current simulation, but now also best suited for non-linear steady state load flow calculations. More detail can be read in the file named mathematical model description point doc.

There the individual models are explained in detail. By disassembling a complex matrix into two real valued ones, the individual elements such as the voltage source load, overhead line, cable, and so on have twice as many connections. For example, a overhead line has now four connections instead of two.

Two connections form the real part of the matrix of the element, and the other two connections form the imaginary part of the matrix of the element. The real connected elements have a cross in symbol output pin to better recognize them. It is important that only the elements with a cross may be connected to each other and the elements without a cross may also be connected to each other.

Mixing is not allowed. Now to the circuit structure and the simulation. A circuit with three nodes is to be simulated In this example. It is assumed that the Ltspice simulator has already been configured as shown in my YouTube video and maybe even experimented a little with the surface. The LTspice simulator was originally developed for electronic simulations, but now with my models work also very well with load flow calculations and transient high voltage simulations.

And now for the generation of the three bus circuit, a template is loaded and immediately saved to a new name.

Certain items necessary for the simulation are already predefined in this template. For example, the frequency is 50 hertz. The voltage factor is root three, and the power factor is three. The voltage factors and power factor are required for single phase simulation in order to correctly calculate the three phase parameters such as active power, reactive power, and voltage.

PQV, the parameters vntol and abstol are the voltage and current tolerances for the simulation. In this case, one millivolt and one micro amp.  These values can be increased if necessary in the event of non convergence of the simulation. The default simulation type is transient analysis for the time domain.

The slack ensures that the power balance of the system is always fulfilled. The sum of the energy supplied to the circuit and the energy consumed in the circuit is therefore always zero.

A few more words about the PV element. A PV element models a synchronous generator with fixed active power output and voltage regulation. The voltage regulation is achieved by changing the excitation voltage, which is accompanied by a change in reactive power. The sum of active power and reactive power results in the apparent power  i.e the design point of the synchronous generator.

I now change the PV element back to the original values for active power and reactive power limit.

I am now interested in the currents over the lines. I right click on the waveform viewer window and can now select the two currents from line 12.

To do this, I open a second plot. In the right click menu item, add plot above and move the currents from plot one to plot two. The current i1_m is the current from the start of the line into the line, and the current i2_m is the current at the end of the line. The start of the line is indicated by a small slanted line in the lower part of the overhead line symbol.

The two currents are different because some current from the line still flows into the ground via the capacitances. Also interesting are the omic losses of the line and the reactive power balance of the line. Does the line act as a capacitor or does the inductive component predominate due to high currents flowing through the line?

To find out, I delete plot one and create a new plot with p_vm and q_vm above it.

As you can see, PVM is almost zero and q_vm is approximately minus 13 megawatts. The negative sign means that the line has a reactive power surplus i.e. it feeds reactive power into the grid. If the line were to carry more and more current, the inductive component of the line would consume more and more of the surplus.

The omic power loss of the line is almost zero. Now I look at the voltage angle of bus two. I see that it's negative. The voltage angle of the slack is zero. Active power always flows from the higher to the lower voltage angle. The voltage angle of bus three is the smallest of all three busbar because the active power flows from both lines to the load.

The same applies to the voltage level here. The reactive power always flows from the higher voltage to the lower voltage. The voltage of busbar three is the lowest.
